    Floor-plate massing¶

    The massing stage turns the carved envelope into stacked floor plates: a ground floor, then floors of equal height, each a closed prism whose outline follows the maximum volume's footprint and steps back where the envelope does not allow the floor. Use it for massing models, floor-area figures and as simulation input (one closed prism per floor and plate).

    How plates are formed¶

    1. Footprint and facades. The footprint is the maximum volume's plan projection (the area it covers seen from above), so an uneven or raised base does not change it. Preprocessing saves this footprint (footprint.json in its folder, world coordinates) from the mesh it voxelized; massing reads that file, not the mesh in the config passed to massing and not the mesh file on disk, so later edits to either change nothing. If preprocessing could not compute a footprint, massing uses the outline of the occupied voxel columns and warns. The footprint outline is split into facades wherever it turns by more than 30 degrees. Curved or faceted outlines with smaller turns stay one facade. A facade whose vertices all lie within 1 cm of the line from its first to its last vertex is straight: footprints cut from meshes carry vertices micrometres off a straight wall. Far from the origin, where a mesh stored in float32 rounds its coordinates by more, the tolerance is four of those float32 steps, and it is never more than half a voxel. A real bend, a few degrees over a few metres, stays a bend.
    2. Faces. Every footprint column belongs to its nearest facade. Facades shorter than two modules (2 * min_plate_dimension: chamfers, small steps) get no face of their own; their neighbours meet at one corner across them. At corners the longer facade takes the corner square (up to half the building's depth behind it), so every face ends in a line perpendicular to its facade. At equal lengths (a square) the facade closer to the x axis takes the corner. Along a facade not aligned with the voxel grid, the voxelizer also fills columns whose centre lies just outside the footprint; such a column belongs to the face of the footprint point nearest to it, so the end of a slab is cut on the same lines as the rest of it.
    3. Bays. Each face is cut into square modules of min_plate_dimension (3 m): along its facade, perpendicular to it, and in depth behind it, parallel to it. Along a straight facade the rows of modules are laid from both ends of the building (its extent along the facade) towards the middle; what is left over widens the middle row, or the two middle rows equally (a 10 m bar: 3, 4 and 3 m; 13 m: 3, 3.5, 3.5 and 3 m). An end wing therefore starts with a whole row whatever the building's length, and the cuts on the two sides of a straight building line up. Along a curved facade the rows are cut every module of length from its start. A row narrower than a module at the end of a face that ends inside the building (at a corner square) joins its neighbour, and a module at the back of a row (where the face meets the one opposite) shallower than min_plate_dimension joins the one in front of it. Each module is built on its own, so a wing keeps its floors whatever the envelope does behind it in the same face, for example a courtyard of a perimeter block carved lower. Behind any facade a plate can therefore be as shallow as one module, min_plate_dimension.
    4. Floors. For each floor (ground floor ground_floor_height, then floor_height), a module is built when it stays inside the maximum volume over the floor's full height, at most plate_tolerance of its volume lies outside the carved envelope, and (with plate_stacking: supported) it is built on the floor below. A column along a facade not aligned with the voxel grid counts only for its share inside the footprint, so whether a module is built does not depend on the angle of the footprint to the grid; a column less than half inside the footprint need not be filled by the maximum volume at all (along a wall the voxels can reach up to a voxel past the footprint, as the re-voxelized envelope of an earlier carving does). A module over the tolerance is not built even when built modules enclose it, so a local dip in the envelope can leave a hole of one or two modules in a floor; holes are not filled. Parts narrower than min_plate_dimension are dropped (the test is the same at any angle of the footprint to the voxel grid), so a strip one module deep, min_plate_dimension, is kept. The ground floor starts at the maximum volume's lowest point, and the top floor ends at or below its highest point; preprocessing records both in its manifest. The voxel grid is not aligned with these points, so the lowest occupied voxel layer can lie mostly below the base (its centre below it) and the highest mostly above the roof. Such a layer is filled only where rays hit the base or the roof, which they miss in columns along facades that are not aligned with the grid, so it does not count: a floor's height and its share outside the envelope are measured over the layers whose centre lies between the lowest and the highest point. A preprocessing run from before the manifest recorded these points starts the ground floor at the bottom of the lowest occupied layer and lets the top floor reach the top of the highest occupied layer, up to one voxel outside the maximum volume at each end; running preprocessing again gives the floors above.
    5. Plates. The connected built modules of a floor form a plate. A module on a straight facade is drawn as a rectangle in its facade's frame: cuts lie on the cut lines (along the facade and in depth), the back of the last module lies on the midline where the facade faces a parallel one, and the facade edges are the footprint's own. Other edges (face ends at corners) are within half a voxel of the voxel columns. Where a corner is not square, the faces of its two facades meet along the bisector of the corner; a module there is its rectangle cut by that line (or by a line of the two facades' corner squares, or by a bisector with a facade that meets one of them; where the faces meet along a curve, by the line midway between the two faces' columns turned to a direction of the two facades or their bisector), so it ends on one straight slanted edge. A cut that would leave an angle under 30 degrees or a piece narrower than a voxel is not used. Modules on curved facades, and the modules no such line separates from a neighbouring face, follow their voxel columns, straight to within half a voxel; they stay off the columns of modules not in the plate. Where the shapes of two modules overlap, the overlap belongs to one of them, so plates of different modules never overlap. Spikes and cracks of no width that the boolean operations leave are removed.

    Settings¶

    Setting Default Meaning
    ground_floor_height 5.0 Height of the ground floor (m)
    floor_height 3.0 Height of every floor above it (m)
    min_plate_dimension 3.0 Side of the square module and minimum plate width (m); "Grid module" in the browser UI
    plate_tolerance 0.2 Largest share of a module's volume on a floor outside the carved envelope; "Permitted volume exceedance" in the browser UI
    plate_stacking supported supported: build a module only on a built module; free: each floor on its own

    Plates never leave the maximum volume. The three sizes must be at least the voxel size of the preprocessing run; the stage reports an error naming the setting otherwise.

    Outputs¶

    In <out_dir>/massing/:

    • plates.json: one entry per plate (floor, z bottom and top, outline and holes in world coordinates, area, number of modules (bays), share of its volume outside the envelope), plus the floor area per floor and in total;
    • plates.obj: one closed mesh per plate, named floor_<n>_plate_<m>, written whatever final_mesh_format says (the browser interface draws it and reads its parts by name);
    • plates.ply, plates.stl or plates.glb when final_mesh_format is one of them: the same plates, as one mesh in PLY and STL (formats without named parts) and with one named part per plate in GLB. The manifest names this file as export_mesh_path (plates.obj itself for OBJ). A change of final_mesh_format reruns massing as well as exporting. Far from the origin (georeferenced models) PLY keeps the coordinates in double precision and GLB places the offset in each node, while STL can only hold float32 and the run reports a float32_coordinates issue when that rounds them by more than 1 mm;
    • manifest.json and diagnostics/diagnostic.json (floors built, plates, floor areas, share of the carved envelope inside plates, volume placed outside the envelope through the tolerance, footprint source).

    The run report gets a Massing section.

    Running it¶

    • Python: massing(thr_result, cfg, out_dir)
    • CLI: usc massing -c config.yaml (finds the thresholding manifest of the run, or pass -f)
    • Grasshopper: USC_Massing after USC_ThresholdStage, with USC_MassingSettings

    Limits¶

    • Floors count from the lowest point of the maximum volume; a part that starts higher gets a warning (sloped ground is not supported yet).
    • If the maximum volume's footprint cannot be read from the mesh, or does not match the voxel grid (more than 5% of the occupied voxel columns more than one voxel outside it, for example when the file was replaced after preprocessing), the voxel footprint is used, with a warning; plates can then be up to one voxel larger than the true footprint.
    • Modules are rectangles in their face's frame; there are no interior zones (core, perimeter) and roofs are flat.
