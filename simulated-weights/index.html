    Simulated Benefit Weights¶

    Benefit mode's optional simulated weighting runs a small building simulation inside USC. A simulation brings physical assumptions that the geometric parts of USC do not have, so this page documents the engine, its origin, how it was validated, what the user supplies, and where its limits are.

    The engine¶

    USC implements the ISO 13790 Annex C simple hourly method, commonly called the 5R1C model: a single thermal zone represented by five conductances and one lumped thermal-mass capacitance.

    • Nodes: outdoor air, supply air, indoor air, an internal surface node, and the thermal mass.
    • Conductances: ventilation, air-surface coupling, glazing, surface-mass coupling, and the opaque-envelope remainder, all derived per the standard (coupling coefficients h_is = 3.45 W/m²K, h_ms = 9.1 W/m²K, Λ_at = 4.5; thermal mass per ISO 13790 Table 12 mass classes).
    • Time stepping: Crank-Nicolson on the mass node, one hour per step, with the standard's three-step heating/cooling demand calculation (free-float, 10 W/m² test load, exact linear interpolation to the setpoint).

    On top of the simulation sits perturbation attribution: for every hour of the year, the transmitted solar gain of that hour is perturbed by ±1 W and the change in annual heating and cooling demand is attributed to that hour (central differences). This yields two hourly series in [0, 1]:

    Series Meaning
    benefit[t] fraction of a marginal joule of solar gain at hour t that offsets heating over the year
    harm[t] fraction that becomes cooling load

    Attributing against the annual totals captures thermal lag: a noon gain stored in the mass and released into the cold evening still earns heating credit. All 17,521 year-simulations (the base run plus two per hour) run as one vectorized NumPy batch, in seconds. A run perturbed at hour t has the base run's inputs until then, so it is computed only from hour t on, starting from the base run's state (about half the work, the same numbers); the base run is the batch's first row, which also gives the outcome the artifact records.

    Free running¶

    The zone can also run free (archetype key operation: free_running; the browser app always uses it): no heating or cooling system, only passive performance. The two set points become the limits of a comfort band (t_set_heating to t_set_cooling), and the windows open for natural ventilation:

    • In each hour the air temperature is evaluated with the windows closed and with them fully open (vent_ach extra air changes per hour, default 4). When the closed air would be above vent_above (default 25 °C) and opening cools it, the windows open as far as needed to bring the air to vent_above, at most fully; the hour's air and mass temperatures are interpolated between the two evaluations by that share. They stay shut when outdoors is warmer.
    • The year starts from a periodic steady state: the mass temperature at the end of a first year run from 20 °C, since a free-running zone's mass keeps the winter's state for weeks.
    • The outcome is in degree-hours: in each hour, how many degrees the air is below the band (underheating) or above it (overheating), added over the year (K·h).

    The attribution perturbs each hour's solar gain by ±10 W (a larger step than the conditioned ±1 W, because degree-hours change in steps where the air crosses a limit of the band) and gives:

    Series Meaning
    benefit[t] degree-hours below the band that one more watt-hour of sun at hour t removes over the year, × the zone's heat loss coefficient H
    harm[t] degree-hours above the band that it adds, × H

    The factor H (W/K: windows, opaque fabric and the mean ventilation, windows closed, simulated_weights.loss_coefficient) makes the series a share of the watt-hour in [0, 1], as the conditioned ones are. In steady state a watt-hour that all goes into warming a room below the band removes 1/H degree-hours, which counts 1; one that arrives while the room is already in the band counts 0, until the mass carries it into a colder hour. Benefit minus harm is a comfort balance in one unit. The functions are simulate_free_running and attribute_free_running.

    Origin and validation¶

    The engine is an independent implementation, written directly from the ISO 13790 Annex C equation set in NumPy (urbansolarcarver/simulated_weights.py). It is not a fork, port, or adaptation of an existing simulator.

    It is cross-validated against RC_BuildingSimulator, the ETH Zurich Architecture & Building Systems group's MIT-licensed implementation of the same annex (Jayathissa et al., "Optimising building net energy demand with dynamic BIPV shading", Applied Energy 202, 2017). The validation setup:

    • Three archetype scenarios: medium office, heavy masonry, light insulated.
    • Weather: the bundled Golden, CO TMY3 EPW (dry-bulb direct; transmitted solar from Ladybug's directional irradiance on each scenario's glazing, g = 0.6).
    • Compared quantities: annual heating and cooling demand totals (agreement to roughly 1 Wh/year, relative tolerance 1e-6), mean annual air temperature, and hourly air-temperature, mass-temperature, and heating/cooling-demand values at sampled hours across the year.
    • Method: the oracle's own derived conductances are fed into USC's engine directly, so the comparison isolates the hourly recurrence itself rather than parameter-derivation conventions (the two implementations intentionally differ on one of those: RC_BuildingSimulator uses U·A directly for the opaque-envelope conductance, USC applies the standard's serial split).
    • Artifacts: the oracle's driving arrays and reference outputs are committed verbatim in tests/data/oracle_5r1c_reference.json; the regression runs in the test suite on every change (tests/test_simulated_weights.py), deterministically, with no weather generation at test time.

    Beyond the oracle comparison, the engine is checked for steady-state energy-balance closure against an independent hand-derived network reduction (constant outdoor temperature, no gains: demand converges to conductance × ΔT with the air node held at setpoint), and for the physical property that heavier mass classes flatten hour-to-hour mass-temperature variation.

    The attribution is checked on the same scenarios: winter hours have a high benefit and no harm, summer afternoons a high harm and a benefit near 0, a winter noon keeps a high benefit (the lag credit), and a heavier mass class gives flatter weights over the day. The hook into benefit mode is checked in tests/test_modes.py and tests/test_pipeline.py: a schedule of all ones gives the plain irradiance weights, a schedule of the balance-point rule's ones and zeros gives the simple weights exactly, and include_harm gives clip(benefit − harm, 0). The script that captured the oracle data is tests/data/capture_oracle_5r1c.py (its paths to the oracle are machine-specific).

    On the demo site (a CUDA workstation, RTX 3060), the simulated weights move about 9 % of the sky's weight relative to the simple rule: less to the east-morning sky, more to the south-west afternoon, the sign of thermal lag. With include_harm the shift reaches 20 to 30 % and gathers in the low southern sky. Tutorial 5 (examples/5_benefit_5r1c.ipynb) reproduces the comparison.

    The model in detail¶

    Conductances (W/K)¶

    Conductance Value Joins
    H_ve, ventilation 1200 · b_ek · V · ACH_tot / 3600, with b_ek = 1 − (ACH_vent / ACH_tot) · η_hr (heat recovery, ISO 13790 Annex E) supply air (= outdoor air, no preheating) and indoor air
    H_tr,is h_is · A_tot, h_is = 3.45 W/m²K, A_tot = Λ_at · A_f, Λ_at = 4.5 indoor air and surface
    H_tr,w Σ U_w · A_w (glazing, no mass) outdoor air and surface
    H_tr,ms h_ms · A_m, h_ms = 9.1 W/m²K surface and mass
    H_tr,em 1 / (1/H_tr,op − 1/H_tr,ms) (the opaque envelope less its surface part, §12.2.2) outdoor air and mass

    A_f is the floor area and V the volume. RC_BuildingSimulator takes H_tr,em = U_op · A_op directly; the validation feeds both engines the same values, so the recurrence is compared, not this convention.

    The mass classes of ISO 13790 Table 12, per m² of floor:

    Class A_m C_m (J/K)
    very light 2.5 · A_f 80,000 · A_f
    light 2.5 · A_f 110,000 · A_f
    medium 2.5 · A_f 165,000 · A_f
    heavy 3.0 · A_f 260,000 · A_f
    very heavy 3.5 · A_f 370,000 · A_f

    With mass_capacity_kj_m2k instead of a class, C_m = capacity · 1000 · A_f, and A_m is that of the class nearest in capacity (the lighter one on a tie).

    Gains and the hourly step¶

    The internal gains Φ_int and the transmitted solar gains Φ_sol are split between the nodes (C.1 to C.3):

    Φ_ia = 0.5 · Φ_int                                          to the air
Φ_m  = (A_m / A_tot) · (0.5 · Φ_int + Φ_sol)                to the mass
Φ_st = (1 − A_m / A_tot − H_tr,w / (9.1 · A_tot))
       · (0.5 · Φ_int + Φ_sol)                              to the surface

    The heating or cooling power Φ_HC enters the air node. With the auxiliary conductances (C.6 to C.8)

    H_tr,1 = 1 / (1/H_ve + 1/H_tr,is)
H_tr,2 = H_tr,1 + H_tr,w
H_tr,3 = 1 / (1/H_tr,2 + 1/H_tr,ms)

    the mass node steps by Crank-Nicolson over one hour (C.4, C.5):

    Φ_m,tot = Φ_m + H_tr,em · θ_e
        + H_tr,3 · (Φ_st + H_tr,w · θ_e + H_tr,1 · ((Φ_ia + Φ_HC) / H_ve + θ_sup)) / H_tr,2

θ_m,t = [θ_m,t−1 · (C_m/3600 − 0.5 · (H_tr,3 + H_tr,em)) + Φ_m,tot]
      / [C_m/3600 + 0.5 · (H_tr,3 + H_tr,em)]

    The hour's mass temperature is the mean θ_m = (θ_m,t + θ_m,t−1) / 2 (C.9), and the surface and air follow (C.10, C.11):

    θ_s   = [H_tr,ms · θ_m + Φ_st + H_tr,w · θ_e + H_tr,1 · (θ_sup + (Φ_ia + Φ_HC) / H_ve)]
      / [H_tr,ms + H_tr,w + H_tr,1]
θ_air = [H_tr,is · θ_s + H_ve · θ_sup + Φ_ia + Φ_HC] / [H_tr,is + H_ve]

    A conditioned zone's demand in each hour takes three steps (C.4.2):

    1. The zone runs without heating or cooling (Φ_HC = 0). If the air stays between the set points, there is no demand.
    2. Otherwise a test load Φ_HC,10 = 10 W/m² · A_f is applied, and the demand is interpolated to the set point that was crossed: Φ_HC,nd = Φ_HC,10 · (θ_set − θ_air,0) / (θ_air,10 − θ_air,0).
    3. The system's capacity is unlimited.

    The heating demand adds the positive Φ_HC,nd over the year, the cooling demand the negative ones.

    With an hourly ach_vent, H_ve changes each hour, and the time loop takes each hour's H_ve with the constants that depend on it. Heat recovery applies to that hour's ventilation, and infiltration stays one value. 8,760 equal values give the result of one value exactly.

    Archetype details¶

    In the shoebox form, expand_shoebox() derives the floor area, the volume, the window area per facade and the opaque area: the walls less their windows, plus the roof and floor when exposed. A part that is not exposed is adiabatic. With u_wall, u_roof and u_floor, H_tr,op = u_wall · opaque walls + u_roof · roof + u_floor · floor, each U needed only when its part is exposed. Giving both u_opaque and u_wall is an error, and so is giving both mass_class and mass_capacity_kj_m2k.

    The shading factors multiply each window's Φ_sol. shading_hot applies during hot_months or during shading_hot_period: [first_day, last_day] (days 1 to 365, both included, wrapping the new year when the first is later), one or the other. The attribution is scaled by each hour's effective transmission, f_eff(t) = Φ_sol,shaded / Φ_sol,unshaded, so the weights stay a share of a watt-hour of exterior sun. The transmitted solar comes from the weather file through Ladybug's directional irradiance on each window, Φ_sol(t) = Σ A_w · g · I(t); only the hourly weights leave the generator, and USC's sky patches never see these areas.

    Attribution¶

    All 1 + 2 × 8,760 = 17,521 runs (the base year and a +ε and a −ε run for each hour) march together as one NumPy batch. Each run carries one state, its mass temperature, and the heating and cooling branches are masks. The values are marginal, at the archetype's operating point. Near a switch between heating, free floating and cooling the derivative is a one-sided one, which the central differences smooth. The series are clipped to [0, 1], with a warning when a value lies more than 0.02 outside.

    The weights file¶

    usc simulate-weights (generate_simulated_weights) writes simulated_weights.json, with a .png beside it that shows benefit and harm as day × hour charts:

    {
  "schema_version": 2,
  "meta": {
    "method": "iso13790-5r1c-perturbation",
    "operation": "conditioned",
    "epw": "<the weather file as given>",
    "archetype": { "...the archetype, expanded..." },
    "eps_w": 1.0,
    "heating_kwh_m2": 0.0,
    "cooling_kwh_m2": 0.0,
    "sun_up": [8760 values, 0 or 1],
    "generated": "<ISO timestamp>",
    "generator": "usc simulate-weights (iso13790-5r1c)"
  },
  "hourly": {
    "benefit": [8760 values in [0, 1]],
    "harm":    [8760 values in [0, 1]]
  }
}

    The 8,760 values are the hours of a non-leap year, index = hour of the year. A free-running zone has method iso13790-5r1c-free-running-perturbation, eps_w 10, and in place of the two demands underheating_kh, overheating_kh, hours_below, hours_above, comfort_band_c and loss_coefficient_w_k. sun_up marks the hours with the sun above the horizon at the weather file's site, at the middle of each hour (sun_up_hours()), so a reader can tell a night hour from a day hour whose weight is 0. Files of schema version 1, and version 2 files that still hold the rooms per facade direction of 30 October 2026, are read for their hourly.benefit and harm.

    How benefit mode uses it¶

    With simulated_weights_path set, each hour's direct and diffuse radiation in the weather data is scaled by benefit[t] before the cumulative sky matrix is built. The sky integration then spreads each hour's weight over the patches that hour's sun and sky occupied; the cold-hours rule is the special case of weights 0 and 1. include_harm: true subtracts the matrix scaled by harm[t], clipped at 0. balance_temperature and balance_offset are not used, with a warning if they were changed. The file is read again on every preprocessing run, and its meta goes into the preprocessing diagnostics. With diagnostic_plots: true, the diagnostics also hold the comparison dome: the difference between these weights and the simple rule's for the same config (sky_patch_comparison_image, with the shift in diagnostic.json).

    What you supply: the archetype¶

    The archetype describes a typical building in about ten numbers. Two equivalent forms are accepted (configs/archetype_example.yaml shows both):

    Shoebox form (areas derived for you):

    Key Meaning
    width, length, height zone dimensions (m); floor area, volume, facade areas derived
    wwr window-to-wall ratio per facade, keyed north/east/south/west
    g_value glazing solar transmittance (applies to all windows)
    orientation optional rotation of the whole box, degrees clockwise from north

    Explicit form: floor_area, volume, area_opaque, area_window, and a windows list of [azimuth_deg, area_m2, g_value], or [azimuth_deg, area_m2, g_value, altitude_deg] for a window that is not vertical (the altitude of its normal: 90 for a horizontal skylight).

    Both forms additionally take: u_opaque, u_window (W/m²K), ach_vent, ach_infiltration (1/h), heat_recovery (0-1), mass_class (very_light … very_heavy, ISO 13790 Table 12), t_set_heating, t_set_cooling (°C), and internal gains as either a flat internal_gains_w_m2 (W/m², default 5) or a 24-value daily profile internal_gains_profile_w_m2.

    The shoebox form may instead give u_wall with u_roof and u_floor (W/m²K) and the switches roof_exposed (default true) and floor_exposed (default false): the walls, and the roof and floor when exposed, each at its own U; u_opaque keeps its meaning (walls and roof at one U). The thermal mass may be mass_capacity_kj_m2k (kJ/m²K of floor) instead of mass_class; internal_gains_w_m2 and ach_vent also take 8,760 hourly values (a schedule), and the summer shading may run over shading_hot_period: [first_day, last_day] (days of the year, wrapping the new year when the first is later) instead of hot_months. Archetype details gives the rules.

    How the zone runs (optional): operation is conditioned (the default: ideal heating and cooling to the set points) or free_running (Free running: the set points are the comfort band), and a free-running zone takes vent_above (°C, default 25) and vent_ach (1/h, default 4) for its windows. These two keys belong to a free-running zone only; with a conditioned one they are refused.

    Shading coefficients (optional, declared multipliers; no device geometry is modelled):

    Key Meaning
    shading_permanent year-round solar transmission multiplier in [0, 1] (fins, context obstructions); 1 = no shading
    shading_hot additional multiplier applied only during hot_months (awnings, tents, seasonal vegetation); stacks with the permanent factor
    hot_months calendar months (1-12) when shading_hot is deployed, e.g. [6, 7, 8, 9]

    Each coefficient accepts a scalar (all facades) or a per-facade mapping ({south: 0.8, west: 0.9}); windows are assigned to their nearest cardinal facade. The perturbation attribution is attenuated by the same factors, so the weights describe a marginal joule of exterior solar: a July joule arriving while the awning is out counts for little. The declared coefficients are recorded in the artifact's provenance. Note that a fixed overhang's blocking is concentrated in high-sun hours, so its effect is usually best expressed through shading_hot rather than shading_permanent.

    The shipped example values are deliberately neutral, with no national defaults. For real projects, source values from TABULA (EU building-stock typologies), DOE prototype buildings (US), or your national code tables.

    The usc archetype command builds and validates the file for you, printing the derived floor area, volume, and per-facade window areas so mistakes surface before any simulation runs:

    usc archetype -o my_building.yaml -s width=12 -s wwr_south=0.4 -s mass_class=heavy
usc archetype --from my_building.yaml -o variant.yaml -s mass_class=light   # derive a variant

    In Grasshopper, USC_Archetype assembles the archetype from five family feeder components (USC_ZoneGeometry / Fabric / Ventilation / Operation / Shading, one wire each), feeding USC_SimulateWeights, whose output path wires into USC_BenefitParams. Its room runs free unless USC_ZoneOperation's free_running is False, which also takes vent_above and vent_ach; usc archetype and an archetype file without operation give a conditioned zone.

    In the app¶

    In the browser app a Solar heating criterion offers the weights as Which hours count: Energy-weighted (the other choice, Cold hours, is the balance temperature rule). The app describes the model as a simple hourly model of one room, names no standard in its labels and help, and does not use the name 5R1C, which stays the engine's. A project has one typical building, the archetype of every energy-weighted criterion, chosen from a library and edited in a window that opens over the app (Browser UI: the typical building). Its room runs free (Free running): the weights measure passive performance, the sun that keeps the room in its comfort band, not the energy a heating or cooling system would save.

    ISO 13790:2008 has been withdrawn and replaced by EN ISO 52016-1:2017, whose hourly method gives each building element several nodes instead of one lumped mass. The engine keeps the 13790 method, the one validated against RC_BuildingSimulator.

    The values of the sheet¶

    The sheet's values (local_web_service/typical_building.py, archetype_of) become a shoebox archetype:

    Sheet Archetype
    Hours in use: Residential (6:00 to 24:00 every day), Office (8:00 to 18:00, Monday to Friday) or Always the hours in which the internal gains and the fresh air apply, as 8,760-value internal_gains_w_m2 and ach_vent (0 outside them); Always gives one value each. The weekday of 1 January is the weather file's (its DATA PERIODS line; Monday when it gives none)
    Comfort band, from and to; heat recovery t_set_heating, t_set_cooling (the band's limits), heat_recovery; operation: free_running
    Internal gains while in use, W/m² internal_gains_w_m2 in the hours in use
    Fresh air while in use, m³/h per m² of floor ach_vent = fresh air / storey height in the hours in use
    Infiltration, air changes per hour ach_infiltration, in every hour
    Open the windows above, °C; open windows let in at most, air changes per hour vent_above, vent_ach
    U of the walls, of the windows; whole-window g u_wall, u_window, g_value
    Thermal capacity, kJ/m²K of floor mass_capacity_kj_m2k; the effective mass area is that of the ISO 13790 class nearest in capacity
    Summer shading: one factor and a period of dates shading_hot with shading_hot_period (days of the year); none outside it
    Room the shoebox width, length, height, orientation, wwr
    Roof and floor, exposed or not, each with its U roof_exposed, u_roof, floor_exposed, u_floor

    The three cases of use are examples of hours, not an editor: gains and fresh air are on in the hours of the case and off outside them, with no holidays. The project's values (project.json, the library's YAML files) use the keys of typical_building.VALUE_KEYS: schedule (residential, office, always), t_set_heating, t_set_cooling, gains_w_m2, fresh_air_m3h_m2, heat_recovery, u_wall, u_window, g_value, mass_capacity_kj_m2k, shading_factor (null for none), shading_start, shading_end ("MM-DD"), room (average with depth, or box with width, length, orientation), height, wwr, roof_exposed, u_roof, floor_exposed, u_floor, ach_infiltration, vent_above, vent_ach (a building stored before they existed reads as 25 °C and 4 per hour).

    The default room. The weights do not depend on the size of the room, only on its ratios: facade and window per m² of floor, volume per m² of floor. The default room ("average") is a square plan turned 45° with all four walls exposed and the same window share on each, so its windows face NE, SE, SW and NW and no direction is favoured. Its side is four times the room depth, which gives it the facade per m² of floor of a room that deep with one facade: 6 m deep is a 24 × 24 m square. An average room of depth d is the box of width and length 4d at orientation 45° whose north, east, south and west shares are the NE, SE, SW and NW ones. The explicit box (width, length, orientation, a share per wall) is the other kind of room.

    Roof and floor are adiabatic by default: the room stands for a storey between others, and no heat passes through its ceiling and floor. A top storey or a floor over pilotis is turned on under Advanced, Roof and floor, with its own U.

    The weights describe one room with the mean exposure of the building's facades: they are the same for every facade of the carved volume within an hour (the time of day carries the direction of the sun), so a Wh of sun is worth the same on a south facade and on a north one in the same hour. A facade's direction enters through how much sun it receives from each sky patch: in benefit mode a ray counts its patch weight times the cosine of its angle to the surface (Carving Modes), so a north facade, which receives little, constrains a massing little. What lies behind a test surface is not known, so no room per facade is assumed.

    The library¶

    The library is apart from projects: the built-in examples, read-only, and the user's own entries, one YAML file each in ~/.urbansolarcarver/typical_buildings/ (beside the user preferences file), so an own entry serves every projects folder. A project keeps its own copy of the values and the name of the entry it came from; changing or deleting the entry does not change the project, and the sheet says "Changed from" the entry once the copy differs.

    A new project has no typical building until one is chosen from the library: the box in the criterion's Settings says "None chosen", and a run with an energy-weighted criterion that is on is refused as a missing input (missing_inputs names typical_building) until one is chosen. A building stored with the keys of an earlier form of the values (before 2026-10-29) reads as none chosen, and an own entry with such keys is left out of the library.

    The examples are grouped by the source of their values; the values come only from these examples, and the form offers no lists of standard values.

    Calculate¶

    Calculate in the window runs generate_simulated_weights with the project's weather file into inputs/typical_building/weights.json (with weights.png) and shows Effect of sunlight, by hour: a day-by-hour chart of benefit minus harm when the criterion has include_harm (red where the sun offsets heating, blue where it adds to cooling), of benefit alone otherwise, with the hours that have the sun down (meta["sun_up"]) in grey; then the free-running room's degree-hours below and above the comfort band and its hours outside it, which the generator records in the artifact's meta (underheating_kh, overheating_kh, hours_below, hours_above; a file calculated before 2026-10-31 holds the conditioned room's heating_kwh_m2 and cooling_kwh_m2 instead, and the window shows those), and the hours that count. Calculate takes about 5 s; a bar under the button shows how far it has come (generate_simulated_weights reports the share done through its progress argument, by the measured time of each step, about 20 times). The calculation is keyed by a hash of the values, of the method (typical_building.WEIGHTS_METHOD, so the conditioned weights of every building calculated before 2026-10-31 are out of date) and of the weather file: a change of any marks the weights out of date, and Run analysis calculates them again before it analyses an energy-weighted criterion, so a run never uses old weights. The generated config.yaml of such a criterion has simulated_weights_path and no balance_temperature or balance_offset. The explicit-area form, per-facade shading factors and uploading a ready weights file stay in the CLI and Grasshopper.

    Limitations¶

    The weights are a marginal, single-zone, schedule-free physical signal. They capture effects the binary balance-point rule cannot (thermal lag, mass state, gain saturation), but the model makes the following simplifications on purpose:

    • Single zone, lumped mass: the weights are facade-independent within an hour; time-of-day carries the directional signal, and one room stands for the whole building (the app's average room has the mean exposure of its facades). A room behind a south facade that its own sun already warms values more sun less than the averaged room does; rooms per facade direction were tried (2026-10-30) and removed, because what lies behind a test surface is not known and the tool protects the absolute gains each facade receives. Perimeter-zone effects are out of scope for a massing tool.
    • Conditioned: ideal, unlimited conditioning at the air node, at constant set points, no setback. Free running: a fixed comfort band, with degree-hours below and above it counted alike (1 K·h is 1 K·h on either side), and no adaptive comfort. Internal gains and ventilation may follow an hourly schedule (the app's three example cases of use); infiltration and the set points do not.
    • Window opening by rule: a free-running zone opens its windows as far as needed above vent_above when outdoors is cooler, up to vent_ach, in any hour, night included; there is no wind or stack model and no occupant behaviour. A conditioned zone has fixed air-change rates, no night flushing. Neither has adaptive shading control. Without a declared shading_hot coefficient the harm channel is overstated, since real buildings have cheap defenses against summer sun that they lack for missing winter sun; declaring seasonal shading in the archetype makes harm a defensible estimate instead of a worst case. include_harm remains opt-in.
    • Schedules without holidays: the app's example cases of use repeat every week of the year; the office lighting counts its full installed power in every hour of use, with no daylight.
    • Marginal linearization: the weights describe one extra watt at the archetype's operating point; window sizes and g-values in the archetype set where gains saturate, and are recorded in the artifact's provenance metadata.
    • Unshaded derivation: the weights are computed for an unshaded archetype while the carve itself creates shading context; a first-order acceptable inconsistency for massing studies.
    • TMY weather; the ISO coupling coefficients carry the standard's residential-European calibration provenance.

    Treat include_harm as a bracketing scenario

    Without declared seasonal shading, harm is conservatively overstated (in a conditioned zone no night ventilation is modelled either; a free-running zone opens its windows), so the benefit-only weighting is the recommended default. Declaring shading_hot + hot_months in the archetype makes the harm channel a defensible estimate; even then, enable the subtraction deliberately for overheating-critical projects rather than as the standard mode.

    Traceability¶

    Every artifact records its full provenance (meta: method, EPW, the complete expanded archetype, perturbation size, timestamp), and every carve that consumes one records that provenance in its preprocessing diagnostics. The artifact file is read again on every preprocessing run, so a regenerated artifact takes effect on the next run. With diagnostic_plots: true, the run also writes a comparison dome quantifying how the simulated weights shifted the sky relative to the simple rule under the same configuration.
